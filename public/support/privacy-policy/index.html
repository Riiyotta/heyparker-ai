16. Save to Parker Browser Extension

This section describes how the "Save to Parker" Chrome browser extension (the "Extension") collects, uses, and protects your information. The Extension is a separate distribution of Parker that you install from the Chrome Web Store. By using the Extension, you agree to the data handling practices described in this Privacy Policy, including this section. To the extent of any conflict between this section and the rest of this Policy in matters specific to the Extension, this section controls.

16.1 Supported Platforms and Where the Extension Runs

The Extension provides its "Save to Parker" feature on the following surfaces — the only places it injects a Save button, scrapes content, fetches additional metadata, or sends anything to Parker:

  • Facebook Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library)

  • Facebook posts and photos — organic content on facebook.com: post permalinks (facebook.com/<page>/posts/…, /permalink.php, /story.php, /share/p/…), photo pages (/photo/…, /photo.php), your home feed (a small Save button on the photos of feed posts), and the main profile and Page timeline. This includes posts and photos that are public as well as those visible to you within your own signed-in Facebook session. The Extension does not provide its Save feature on Facebook Groups, Events, Marketplace, or Stories.

  • Facebook Reels — the Reels viewer on facebook.com (facebook.com/reel/…), including reels you reach by scrolling the Reels "For You" feed, and reels that appear in your home feed (a small Save button on the reel tile). The Save button is not provided on the profile Reels-tab grid until you open a reel.

  • Facebook video posts — video watch pages (facebook.com/watch?v=…) and profile or Page video pages (facebook.com/<page>/videos/…). The Save button is provided on the video's detail page; a video in your home feed is saved by opening it into its watch page first.

  • Instagram (instagram.com) — posts, reels, carousels, and Stories across your home feed, post and reel permalinks (instagram.com/p/…), profile and Explore grids (which open a post in a dialog), the Reels swipe viewer (instagram.com/reels/…), and the Stories viewer (instagram.com/stories/<account>/…). For carousel (multi-image) posts, the Extension saves the specific slide you are viewing at the moment you click Save; for a Story, it saves the specific story segment you are viewing. The Extension does not provide its Save feature on Instagram Story Highlights (instagram.com/stories/highlights/…), Direct messages (instagram.com/direct/…), or account-settings pages.

  • LinkedIn (www.linkedin.com) — organic posts containing images or video: your home feed (linkedin.com/feed), post permalinks (linkedin.com/feed/update/… and the shareable linkedin.com/posts/… links), and company and member activity tabs (linkedin.com/company/<page>/posts/, linkedin.com/in/<member>/recent-activity/…). Viewing these requires your own signed-in LinkedIn session. The Extension does not provide its Save feature on LinkedIn messaging (linkedin.com/messaging/…), jobs, notifications, My Network, settings, or on text-only posts and document/PDF posts. The public LinkedIn Ad Library (linkedin.com/ad-library/…) is not supported.

  • Pinterest pin detail pages (*.pinterest.com/pin/…) and pin cards on Pinterest feed, board, profile, and search pages, across all country subdomains

  • TikTok (tiktok.com) — video and photo detail pages (tiktok.com/…/video/…, tiktok.com/…/photo/…), and the browsing surfaces where the Save button can be attached to the videos and photo posts you open: your feed (tiktok.com/foryou), Explore (tiktok.com/explore), Search results (tiktok.com/search), hashtag pages (tiktok.com/tag/…), sound pages (tiktok.com/music/…), account profile grids including the Reposts tab (tiktok.com/@<account>), and playlists (tiktok.com/@<account>/playlist/…)

  • YouTube Shorts (youtube.com/shorts/…)

Where the Extension is installed. Because Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube, and Pinterest are single-page applications — the URL changes when you navigate between pages but the browser does not perform a full page load — the Extension's content scripts are registered (per Chrome Web Store manifest declarations) on the full domains facebook.com, instagram.com, www.linkedin.com, tiktok.com, youtube.com, and *.pinterest.com. This is necessary so that the Extension can attach a Save button when you navigate to a supported surface (e.g. when you open a Pinterest pin from the Pinterest homepage without a page refresh).

Transient in-memory observation on the broader install domains. To support single-page-application navigation, the Extension's content scripts run on every page within the six install domains and observe certain network responses your browser already requests as part of normal use of those sites. This observation is transient and in-memory only: the Extension retains a small recent buffer of API responses (capped at 200 entries) whose URLs match a fixed allowlist of paths used by the supported surfaces — specifically /api/graphql/ (Facebook), /graphql/query and /api/v1/ (Instagram), /resource/PinResource/ and /v3/pins/ (Pinterest), /youtubei/v1/ (YouTube), and a small set of TikTok feed, discovery, and detail paths (/aweme/detail, /api/item/detail, /api/post/item_list, /api/preload/item_list, /api/recommend/item_list, /api/explore/item_list, /api/prefetch/explore/item_list, /api/search/item/full, /api/search/general/full, /api/challenge/item_list, /api/music/item_list, /api/mix/item_list, /api/repost/item_list). Responses outside this allowlist are dropped and never retained. On LinkedIn the Extension retains no network responses at all — there is no LinkedIn entry in that allowlist, and nothing LinkedIn's web app requests is buffered. LinkedIn saves work by a different mechanism, described in §16.2. Additionally, on YouTube only, the Extension hooks the browser's Media Source Extensions APIs so that, when you click Save on a YouTube Short, the bytes the browser is already streaming for that Short can be uploaded to your Parker account; this hook is not installed on any other domain.

What the Extension does on non-supported pages within those domains. On any page within the six install domains that is not one of the supported surfaces listed above, the Extension does not inject a Save button, does not scrape page content, and never sends anything to Parker servers. The transient in-memory buffer described above is automatically discarded when the tab is closed or navigated away, and entries beyond the 200-entry cap are evicted as new matching responses arrive. Per §16.4 (Limited Use of User Data), no information from non-supported pages is persisted, transmitted to Parker, shared with third parties, or used for any purpose other than enabling the save action when you later land on a supported surface.

The Extension does not run on, read from, or modify any website outside the six install domains listed above.

16.2 Data the Extension Reads from Supported Platforms

When you visit a supported surface listed in §16.1, the Extension reads:

  • Page metadata visible in your browser: the URL of the post or ad you are viewing, the post's caption or description, the author's display name, handle, and profile picture, publicly displayed engagement counts (likes, comments, views, shares), and the media (images or video) the platform is rendering for you.

  • API responses your browser receives: the Extension passively observes the JSON responses that the platform's web app already requests, in order to identify the specific item you wish to save. Observation is strictly read-only — the Extension does not modify, suppress, or redirect any platform request or response.

  • For Facebook Ad Library specifically: while you have a Facebook Ad Library tab open, the Extension reads two tokens that Facebook places in your page for the page's own scripts to use: a CSRF token (fb_dtsg) and a session token (lsd). Both tokens are read from the GraphQL request bodies that Facebook's own web app sends and from <input> and <script> elements in the page DOM. The Extension only uses these tokens locally, inside your tab, to issue a single authenticated request to Facebook's GraphQL endpoint — fetching the canonical advertiser metadata for the specific ad you are saving — and that request is fired only when you click "Save to Parker" on that ad. No Facebook session tokens or cookies are ever transmitted to Parker servers.

  • For Facebook posts and photos specifically: as with the Ad Library flow above, the Extension reads the same two tokens Facebook places in your page for its own scripts to use — a CSRF token (fb_dtsg) and a session token (lsd) — from the GraphQL request bodies Facebook's own web app sends and from the page DOM. It also observes a set of routine, non-identifying session parameters (roughly twenty technical values such as __rev, __dyn, and jazoest, which Facebook requires on its own GraphQL requests) from the requests your browser already makes during normal use of Facebook. When you click "Save to Parker" on a post or photo and the information already visible in the page is incomplete (common on Facebook's photo-viewer layout, where engagement counts are loaded only into a dialog), the Extension uses these tokens and parameters to issue a single authenticated request to Facebook's GraphQL endpoint — fetching the post's canonical author, caption, engagement counts, and media, the same data Facebook loads to render the post for you. This request is fired only inside your tab, only in direct response to your clicking Save, and only for the specific post or photo you are saving. To issue it, the Extension reuses the opaque query identifiers (doc_id values) Facebook serializes into its own requests, which it caches locally as described in §16.5. No Facebook session tokens or cookies are ever transmitted to Parker servers.

  • For Facebook Reels and video posts specifically: the caption, the author's name and profile picture, and the engagement counts Facebook exposes (likes, comments, and shares; video posts additionally carry a view count — Facebook does not provide one for reels), together with the video and audio, are read from the responses Facebook's own web app has already streamed for the page you are viewing. When you save a reel and that data is not already present in the responses your browser received (common when saving a reel directly from your home feed), the Extension uses the same tokens and routine session parameters described above to issue a single authenticated request to Facebook's GraphQL endpoint — fetching that reel's metadata and media, the same data Facebook loads to render the reel. As with posts and photos, this request is fired only inside your tab, only in direct response to your clicking Save, and only for the specific reel you are saving. The media is downloaded from Facebook's public content-delivery network when you click Save. No Facebook session tokens or cookies are ever transmitted to Parker servers.

  • For Instagram specifically: the Extension reads the post, reel, carousel, or story you are saving only from data Instagram's own web app has already loaded — the server-rendered data embedded in the page and the GraphQL and /api/v1/ responses your browser already received during normal use of Instagram. This applies to Stories exactly as it does to posts and reels: the story segment you are viewing is read only from the data already present for the open Story viewer. Unlike the Facebook and Pinterest flows above, the Instagram integration reads no Instagram session token or cookie and issues no additional authenticated request to Instagram on your behalf. When you click "Save to Parker," the media (image or video) you are saving is downloaded directly from Instagram's public content-delivery network, without sending your Instagram cookies.

  • For Pinterest specifically: when you click "Save to Parker" on a Pinterest pin, the Extension reads Pinterest's CSRF token (the csrftoken cookie that Pinterest sets on its own domain) and a public Pinterest application-version identifier from the page, and issues a single authenticated request to Pinterest's /resource/PinResource/get/ endpoint to fetch the canonical pin object for the pin you are saving. This request is made only in direct response to your clicking Save, only inside your tab, only for the specific pin you are saving, and only using the session you have already established with Pinterest. No Pinterest session tokens or cookies are ever transmitted to Parker servers.

  • For YouTube Shorts specifically: the Extension captures the video bytes that your browser is already streaming and decoding to render the Short you are watching. No separate download from YouTube's content delivery network is initiated by the Extension.

  • For LinkedIn specifically: the Extension does not intercept or retain any network response, and it never issues a request of its own to LinkedIn. It reads only what the page you are viewing has already rendered — the post's text, the author's name and profile picture, the publicly displayed reaction, comment and repost counts — together with the page's own already-loaded component state, which is where LinkedIn holds the address of the image or video it is displaying to you. No LinkedIn tokens are read, and no session tokens or cookies are ever transmitted to Parker servers. The media itself is downloaded from LinkedIn's public content-delivery network when you click Save. The Extension performs no action on your behalf on LinkedIn: it cannot react, comment, repost, follow, connect, or message, and it does not click LinkedIn's own controls.

16.3 Data the Extension Sends to Parker

When you click "Save to Parker" and confirm a save, the Extension sends the following to your Parker account:

  • The URL of the saved post or ad.

  • Caption or description text, author display name, author handle, author profile picture, publicly displayed engagement counts, and the platform-specific identifier of the saved item.

  • A copy of the media (image or video) you are saving.

  • The organization, brand, and board you selected in the save dialog, plus any optional notes you typed.

  • Occasionally, up to two Facebook query-catalog identifiers (the non-personal doc_id values described in §16.5), included with a save at most about once a day per identifier as part of the catalog sync described there.

The Extension does not send to Parker any other browsing history, page content from non-supported sites, or platform credentials.

16.5 Browser-Stored Data

The Extension stores the following inside Chrome's chrome.storage.local API on your device:

  • Your Parker access token, refresh token, and token expiry timestamp (used to authenticate with Parker's backend).

  • A locally cached copy of your Parker user profile (account ID, email, display name) and your accessible organizations and brands, used to populate the popup and the save dialog without re-fetching from Parker on every interaction. This cache is refreshed when you click "Refresh connection" in the popup.

  • Your most recently selected organization, brand, and board (so the save dialog defaults are remembered).

  • An offline retry queue containing pending saves that the Extension was unable to deliver due to a temporary network issue (cleared once delivered).

  • A small map of Facebook-issued opaque GraphQL query identifiers (the doc_id values Facebook serializes into its own request URLs — typically 5–20 short numeric strings). These are cached across browser sessions so that, when Facebook rotates its internal query catalog, the authenticated metadata request described in §16.2 does not fail on the first save of a session. These identifiers are not personally identifying and are not derived from your Facebook account or activity — they are versioning labels Facebook assigns to its own query catalog, identical for every Facebook user. Because they are shared technical values, the Extension syncs them with Parker servers in both directions: it periodically fetches the current set from Parker (cached locally) so a fresh install works immediately, and a save request may include up to two of these identifiers that your browser observed (recorded locally so this happens at most about once a day per identifier) so that every user's Extension keeps working when Facebook rotates its catalog. This sync contains no personal data, no browsing history, and no information about your Facebook account.

This data is stored locally and is not synchronized to other devices via Chrome Sync. You can clear all of it at any time by clicking "Disconnect" in the Extension popup, which signs you out and erases all locally stored Extension data.

16.6 Authentication Flow

The Extension authenticates you using the same sign-in methods Parker offers on the web — Google OAuth, or email and password. When you click "Sign In" in the Extension popup, a sign-in window opens to app.heyparker.ai/auth/extension-loginwhere you can choose either method.

If you choose email and password, your credentials are sent over HTTPS to Parker's backend, which validates them against Parker's user database (hosted by our identity provider, Supabase) and immediately discards them. Passwords are never stored, logged, or transmitted by the Extension itself, and Parker's authentication route does not retain or log them beyond the single credential check Supabase performs on the request.

If you choose Google OAuth, you are redirected through app.heyparker.ai and auth.heyparker.ai (Parker's authentication subdomain, hosted by our identity provider) to complete sign-in.

In both cases, on success the Extension receives the same access and refresh tokens it would receive from a sign-in on the web; the Extension stores them locally as described in §16.5.

This server-side fetch sends no cookies and no identifying referrer, is restricted to a fixed allowlist of Meta content-delivery hosts, and discloses nothing about you to the platform.

17. Save from Instagram (Direct Message) Feature

This section describes how the "Save from Instagram" feature collects, uses, and protects your information. The feature lets you save Instagram content to your Parker swipe file by sending it as a Direct Message to a Parker-operated Instagram account — no browser extension required. It relies on the Instagram Platform (specifically the Instagram messaging API) and, in addition to this Policy, is subject to the Meta Platform Terms and the Instagram Platform Policy. This feature is separate from the Save to Parker Browser Extension described in §16; the Extension does not read Instagram Direct messages, and this feature does not involve the Extension.

17.1 How the Feature Works

  • Real Simple Labs operates a dedicated Instagram professional account, @heyparkerdotai (the "Parker Instagram Account").

  • To use the feature, you first link your own Instagram account to your Parker account: from Parker's Settings or the swipe file, you generate a one-time linking code and send it as a Direct Message from your Instagram account to the Parker Instagram Account. Parker records the association between your Instagram account and your Parker account so future messages route to the correct swipe file. Linking is optional and the feature does nothing until you link.

  • Once linked, when you send (DM) an Instagram reel, post, image, or video to the Parker Instagram Account, Parker receives that message through Instagram's messaging API, saves the shared content to your Parker swipe file, and sends you a short confirmation reply in the same conversation. If we receive a message from an Instagram account that is not linked, we reply with linking instructions and do not save the content.

17.2 Information We Receive Through This Feature

When you link your account and send messages to the Parker Instagram Account, we receive from the Instagram Platform:

  • Your Instagram identifiers: the Instagram-scoped user ID for your account and your Instagram username (@handle), used to route your saved content to your Parker account and to display which account is linked.

  • The content you send us: the Instagram post, reel, carousel, image, or video you share, and any accompanying text or caption.

  • Publicly displayed metadata for shared reels: for a shared reel, Parker retrieves publicly displayed information about that reel — the author's display name, handle, and profile picture; the caption and hashtags; publicly displayed engagement counts (likes, comments, views); and the associated audio/music title — so it can be shown alongside the saved idea. For shared posts, images, and other media, Parker retrieves the media itself and its accompanying caption only.

  • A copy of the media (image or video) associated with the shared item, downloaded and stored so the idea remains available after the platform's temporary media links expire.

We do not receive or request your Instagram password, and we do not read your Instagram Direct Message inbox. We receive only the messages you affirmatively send to the Parker Instagram Account, delivered to us by the Instagram Platform via webhook.

17.3 How We Use This Information

  • To save the content you send into the correct Parker swipe file (the organization, brand, and board associated with your link) and to display it with its metadata and media.

  • To send you a confirmation reply — or, if your Instagram account is not yet linked, linking instructions.

  • To operate, secure, and troubleshoot the feature.

Consistent with the Limited Use commitments in §16.4, we use information received through this feature only to provide the save-from-Instagram feature described here. We do not sell it; we do not use it for advertising, including personalized, retargeted, or interest-based advertising; and we do not allow humans to read it except (i) with your explicit consent, including a support request that requires access to the specific data to resolve it; (ii) for security investigations or to comply with applicable law; or (iii) in aggregated, anonymized form for internal operations consistent with this Policy. Our use complies with the Meta Platform Terms and applicable Meta developer policies.

17.4 Storage and Retention

  • The link between your Instagram account and your Parker account, the saved ideas, and the associated media are stored on Parker's infrastructure (Supabase database and file storage; see §4 and §5), with the same security controls described in §4.3.

  • Saved ideas and their media are retained under the retention rules in §4.2 for as long as they remain in your swipe file.

  • The one-time linking codes are short-lived, single-use, and stored only in hashed form.

17.5 Your Controls

  • Unlink your Instagram account at any time from Parker's Settings or the swipe-file connection panel. Unlinking removes the association between your Instagram account and your Parker account, and messages you send afterward are no longer saved.

  • Delete saved ideas individually from the Parker web app; deletion follows the retention rules in §4.2.

  • Submit a data deletion request for your entire Parker account at app.heyparker.ai/privacy/delete-data, which covers data saved through this feature, including the Instagram account link and any content saved via Direct Message.

  • You may also stop using the feature at any time simply by not sending messages to the Parker Instagram Account.

The Instagram Platform delivers your messages to Parker and provides the identifiers and metadata described above; your use of Instagram remains governed by Instagram's and Meta's own terms and privacy policies. Media and metadata retrieval and storage use the same third-party services described in §5 and §16.9 (Supabase for database and file storage; Vercel for hosting). Real Simple Labs is not responsible for how Instagram or Meta logs, retains, or uses the messages you send through their platform.
